How I work

Make it concrete, test it, hand it over.

Most of my work starts inside someone else’s system: an undocumented model, a team that disagrees on what a term means, a process run by hand. I read what exists, agree on what “working” means in numbers, and build the smallest system that meets it.

Then I do the part that decides whether it survives: tests, monitoring, infrastructure as code, and documentation written for the people who will operate it after I leave. I work independently, say what I do not know, and prefer a measured result to a confident claim.
